<Page
  eyebrow="The system"
  title="The surface is a register, not a dashboard."
  lede="Everything else on this page follows from that one sentence. A dashboard shows you numbers. A register records them, states who recorded them, and lets you check. The six moves below are what make the difference visible."
>
  <Section eyebrow="One" title="Paper, and depth by tone.">
    <p>
      The ground is a barely warm paper (`#F3F2EE`). Depth is a step in tone, not a line: the
      sidebar sits one step below the ground, a surface one step above it, a menu or the selected
      row higher still. A table sits on a surface and has no frame of its own.
    </p>
    <p>
      After dark the same steps run in brand navy. The theme is designed, not inverted: the ground is
      `#0A1F44`, never near-black.
    </p>
  </Section>

  <Section eyebrow="Two" title="Navy is the ink and the action.">
    <PullQuote>What writes is what commits.</PullQuote>
    <p>
      No accent colour acts. Body text is navy; the one filled control on a sheet is navy. An
      operator never has to ask which button commits, because only one of them is solid. Poppy and
      cornflower mark and signal on a page — they never fill a control.
    </p>
    <p>
      After dark navy cannot act against a navy ground, so the fill turns paper-white.
    </p>
  </Section>

  <Section eyebrow="Three" title="Gold marks the one thing.">
    <p>
      In each view gold marks one object: the first attention item, a verdict, the highlighted
      value of a chart. In daylight it is an edge or a mark, never text and never a text background;
      after dark gold may be read as text. Selection and focus are cornflower, so gold never has to
      mean "you are here".
    </p>
    <p>
      The seal is the same gold, and that is why it is a brand moment only: sign-in, the brand mark,
      an empty or onboarding state, a licence or certificate seal. In an operational view it never
      sits beside the one thing, and sealing or rotating a key there is an ordinary action.
    </p>
    <SealSpecimen />
    <Rules>
      <Rule>A filled gold dot: the value is recorded and can be verified.</Rule>
      <Rule>A hollow gold ring: a model inferred it, and nothing has vouched for it yet.</Rule>
      <Rule>No mark: nothing is on record. An absence is a statement, so it gets a shape.</Rule>
      <Rule>The mark never carries the meaning alone. The word beside it does.</Rule>
    </Rules>
    <p>
      This is what makes the AI marking systemic instead of decorative. A claim and a fact are drawn
      from the same gold, in two shapes that cannot be mistaken for each other — and the less certain
      one is the *less* decorated one. No gradient, no glow, no sparkle.
    </p>
  </Section>

  <Section eyebrow="Four" title="Every panel has an index column.">
    <IndexSpecimen />
    <p>
      Thirty-eight pixels down the left of every panel, table and drawer, holding an ordinal or a
      letter. It costs real space and it is the system's fingerprint: it is what turns a
      card into an entry, and it gives a row a handle an operator can say out loud.
    </p>
    <p>
      A drawer keeps the ordinal of the row it opened from, so the row and its detail are visibly the
      same object. A failure keeps its ordinal too, tinted — a failed run is still something the
      register recorded.
    </p>
  </Section>

  <Section eyebrow="Five" title="Ask, then act, then record.">
    <p>
      Nothing a model proposes runs before an operator agrees to it. The confirmation states the
      basis, the scope and whether the action can be reversed, and it offers the explanation before
      the action rather than after it.
    </p>
    <p>
      What follows the action is a receipt, not a way back: it reports what happened and carries the
      audit id of the confirmation that authorised it. Undo-first was considered and refused —
      revoking, erasing and sealing cannot be quietly reversed, and a toast that implies they can is
      a lie about the record.
    </p>
    <Verdicts>
      <Verdict result="pass" note="Basis, scope and reversibility, before the action.">
        “Merge entry 07 into entry 03? Basis: same manifest hash. Scope: 2 entries. Reversible: yes,
        until the next seal.”
      </Verdict>
      <Verdict result="fail" note="The scope is hidden and the reversal is implied.">
        “Merged 2 entries. Undo”
      </Verdict>
    </Verdicts>
  </Section>

  <Section eyebrow="Six" title="Few lines, and each one separates something.">
    <p>
      Rules run only between figures in a row and between the rows of a table. A header has no rule
      under it; a section is set apart by space and tone.
    </p>
    <Rules>
      <Rule>Depth is a step in tone, with at most a soft shadow under a surface.</Rule>
      <Rule>Only a detached plane — a menu, a modal, a toast — gets a shadow, and a technical one.</Rule>
      <Rule>No blur behind text. A mono value behind frosted glass has no measurable contrast.</Rule>
      <Rule>Motion runs at one of two durations and no others: 120ms and 200ms, ease-out, one change at a time.</Rule>
    </Rules>
  </Section>
